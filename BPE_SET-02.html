The Bees on the Roof

Paragraph A
Farhan Islam worked as a mid-level accountant in a busy downtown office building, spending most weekdays buried in spreadsheets and quarterly reports. Few of his colleagues knew about the quiet hobby he had cultivated on the building's flat, unused rooftop: three small wooden beehives, carefully assembled over several weekends with instructions found online. Farhan visited the hives early each morning before his shift began, checking on the bees with a calm, methodical patience that felt entirely different from his desk job. He kept the project deliberately low-key, worried that management might object to an unconventional experiment taking place several floors above the parking garage.

Paragraph B
One unusually warm Thursday afternoon, a significant portion of one hive suddenly swarmed, sending a dense cloud of bees drifting down past several office windows before settling briefly on a balcony railing two floors below. Startled employees abandoned their desks, some recording shaky videos on their phones as the insects clustered together in a dark, moving mass. Within hours, footage of the swarm had circulated widely across local social media groups, accompanied by dramatic captions questioning how bees had ended up in the middle of a downtown business district in the first place.

Paragraph C
Building management received a flood of anxious messages from tenants demanding immediate answers. Several employees expressed genuine concern about allergic reactions, while others worried that the incident might damage the building's professional reputation among visiting clients. The property manager scheduled an emergency meeting for the following morning, and rumours quickly spread that the rooftop hives would be dismantled entirely. Maintenance staff were instructed to inspect every rooftop access point, and a temporary notice was posted near the lobby entrance advising staff to remain calm but avoid the upper floors until further notice.

Paragraph D
Farhan spent a sleepless night anticipating the meeting, fully expecting to be reprimanded and ordered to remove the hives immediately. He had never intended to cause alarm and began drafting an apologetic email, prepared to dismantle the entire project rather than risk his job over a hobby. Sitting outside the manager's office the next morning, he rehearsed his explanation several times, uncertain whether any justification would be enough to save the beehives he had grown genuinely attached to over the past year.

Paragraph E
To Farhan's considerable relief, the meeting took an unexpected turn. The company's newly formed sustainability committee had already researched the incident overnight and argued that rooftop beekeeping aligned perfectly with the building's environmental branding goals. Management agreed to keep the hives, provided proper safety barriers were installed, and local news outlets soon ran a favourable story praising the initiative. Within months, the building received a regional sustainability award, and Farhan was invited to lead a small workshop teaching interested colleagues the basics of responsible urban beekeeping.
